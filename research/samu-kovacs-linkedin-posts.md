You're right, and that's exactly why you should do it.

Here's what it actually takes to make one video work:

You research your own niche to understand the channels that are dominating it.

You find the videos already ranking and figure out where the gaps are.

You dig through adjacent niches for outlier video concepts nobody in your space has used yet.

Once you have your video concept, you write the script, nailing the hook, the structure and the CTAs.

Stop optimizing your content for conversions.

Optimize it for consumption instead.

The thing that decides whether someone buys from you isn't your CTA, your landing page, or your positioning.

It's how many hours they spent with your content.

That's the 7 Hour Rule.

On average, someone consumes around 7 hours of your content before they trust you or your company enough to become a customer.

Is it exact? No. It's a ballpark.

But it's important to think about the math behind how you're currently trying to get there.

1 LinkedIn post = 30 seconds

7 hours of consumption = roughly 840 posts

Not 840 posts you publish. 840 posts one specific person actually reads.

You will never get there just from that.

You must incorporate long-form content into the mix.

One 20-minute video is 20 minutes of your prospect's undivided attention, in one go, with a face and a voice attached to it.

Do that 5-10 times and you're most of the way to 7 hours.

So why YouTube? Why not a podcast, webinars, or newsletters?

Those are all great. But YouTube is the only long-form channel that actually has distribution.

- The YouTube algorithm pushes it to people's home feed.
- It ranks on YouTube search
- It ranks on Google
- It gets cited in LLMs

Then you can take that same video and push it out on LinkedIn, in your nurture emails, on your thank you page, in your pre-call sequence.

Every one of those touchpoints is another chunk of the 7 hours.

You can't get that level of distribution with any other type of long form content.

That's exactly why all the fastest growing B2B companies are jumping on YouTube right now.

There's a video ranking #1 for your niche on YouTube right now that you can beat.

And you don't have to be smarter than the person who made it.

Most B2B companies think ranking on YouTube is about outsmarting the algorithm.

It's not.

It's about being the best result for that search.

That's the whole game.

So go type the term your ICP would search into YouTube.

Open the top 3-5 videos that come up.

Then look for these 3 gaps:

1. It's old

If the top video has 2024 in the title, you already have your angle.

Comparison videos, tool breakdowns, "best X platform" videos... these expire very fast.

Nobody wants software recommendations from 2 years ago.

Sometimes just making the up-to-date version of that video is enough to take the spot.

2. It's shallow

Watch the top ranking video.

In most B2B niches, you'll find someone reading off a script, listing 5 obvious things, and never actually telling you how to do any of it.

So if you just go 3x deeper, you win by default.

3. The packaging is bad

Half the videos ranking for B2B terms have a thumbnail that's just text on a plain background.

Or a screenshot of a slide.

If your thumbnail and title actually make someone want to click, you WILL beat them.


That's it. That's the list.

Update it, go deeper, package it better.

Most B2B niches have barely any real competition on YouTube.

So if you can just focus on these 3 things, you'll dominate.

How do you create content with AI that doesn't sound like AI?

The only way I've found to make it work:

1. You give a huge amount of existing content you produced to AI so it can learn your voice, style and takes

2. You give specific direction on the content piece you want to create and your raw thoughts on what you want to cover

3. You ask the AI to write it based on your thoughts + the existing content you gave

This is not the sexy "Claude one-shots my content" type of workflow.

Being an expert doesn't make you good on camera.

They are 2 completely different skills.

Many founders think:

"I know my stuff better than anyone. I'll just hit record and talk."

And it makes sense, because you've been doing this for years. You can talk about your niche for 3 hours straight at a dinner table without blinking.

But then you sit down in front of a camera.

And suddenly you're rambling, restarting the same sentence 14 times, and hating everything you watch back.

The truth is, your whole approach is wrong.

You've been looking at guys like Hormozi or Gary Vee - how they just produce content off-the-cuff all the time.

But what you don't realize is they've been doing this for the last 10 years.

You just started.

Their on-camera skill is 10/10.

Yours is maybe 2-3/10.

They can turn on a camera with zero prep, riff for 25 minutes, and people will watch the whole thing.

You're not there.

And I'm not saying this as an insult - it's just where literally everyone starts.

The best way I found to fix this is to start with structure.

For all of our clients that are starting YouTube with no video experience, we write the scripts for them.

This reduces all of the friction in the recording process, because they know exactly what to say, how to structure it, etc.

Once they record 10-15 videos like that, they have a good foundation and can start experimenting with going off script.

But before that, sticking to scripted videos is the best thing you can do.

4 years. 50+ B2B YouTube channels. 1,000s of leads.

Here's everything I've learned, compressed into one post:

1. Pick your ICP before you pick your topics. Everything downstream breaks if this is blurry.

2. Views are a scoreboard for creators. You're not a creator.

3. 400 views from buyers beats 40,000 views from non-buyers. Every time.

4. Thumbnail and title are one thing, not two. Judge them together or don't judge them at all.

5. Nobody reads a thumbnail. They glance at it. Design it for the glance.

6. 1 to 3 words on the thumbnail. If you need a sentence, the concept is wrong.

7. Steal proven packaging. Never steal the actual content.

8. Your content is the only place where being different pays.

9. Small niche? Focus on search. Big niche? Fight for the home feed too.

10. Search videos are annuities. They generate views and leads for years.

11. Give away the thing you're scared to give away. That's the video that performs best and converts.

12. Nobody has ever bought from you because your editing was good.

13. Editing turns a good video into a great one. It won't turn a bad idea into leads.

14. The first 10 seconds decide the next 10 minutes. Cut the intro.

15. Two CTAs in every video. One casual around the 33% mark, then a longer one at the end.

One line of proof.

Not your background, not your origin story.

"What can I realistically expect from YouTube?"

This is the question I get on almost every sales call.

Here's the no-bs answer:

If you're starting from 0 with no audience anywhere else, expect 2-4 months before the channel really starts generating leads on its own.

But if you already have an audience on LinkedIn, X, or an email list, you can see lead flow in as little as 2-6 weeks.

Because you're funneling people you already have into videos that nurture them, and not waiting on the algorithm.

The second thing is how niche you are.

If your topic is broad, your videos can get 5,000-10,000+ views consistently.

If your ICP is really specific, you might only get 100 to 500 views per video.

And that's okay because 100 of the exact right people is worth more to your business than 5,000 random viewers who will never buy from you.

A small niche doesn't mean your buyers aren't on YouTube.

It just means there are fewer of them, so you obsess over attracting those specific people.

That's why I always say that B2B businesses should never aim to "go viral."

And here's the best part of YouTube:

Your first month will almost always be your slowest.

But YouTube videos are evergreen, so it only compounds from there.

The companies that win on YouTube look at it as an asset they build over 6-12+ months.

That's what it truly takes to turn it into a real moat that:

- establishes you as the authority

- separates you from your competition

- generates leads on autopilot
